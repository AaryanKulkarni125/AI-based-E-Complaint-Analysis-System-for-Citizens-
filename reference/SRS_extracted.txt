1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This document specifies the software requirements for Version 1.0 of the Citizen Grievance Redressal System for Municipal Corporation, a web and mobile-based portal that enables citizens to report civic issues -- such as service delays, corruption, and administrative failures -- directly to the concerned municipal department, and to track those complaints through to resolution. This SRS covers the complete platform, including the citizen-facing grievance-filing application, the officer workflow console, the administrator management panel, and the live reporting/analytics dashboard. It is intended to serve as the authoritative reference for the design, development, and testing of the system.
1.2 Document Conventions
This document follows standard IEEE template conventions for SRS writing:
Mandatory functional and non-functional requirements use explicit terms such as "shall" or "must".
Headings use a numbered hierarchy (e.g., 4.1, 4.1.1).
Individual functional requirements are labeled with a unique tag of the form REQ-<Feature Abbreviation>-<Number> (e.g., REQ-GRV-1) so they can be traced through design, implementation, and test artifacts.
Font styling retains clear distinction between major headings and body content.
1.3 Intended Audience and Reading Suggestions
This document is intended for the following readers:
1. Project supervisor / evaluator (Software Engineering course):
To guide implementation of the MERN-based web/mobile applications, geo-tagged media capture, and department-routing workflow.

To understand feature offerings, escalation rules, and operational flow.
Department heads and ward officers act as prospective administrators and grievance handlers on the platform.
Citizens as end users of the grievance-filing and tracking interface.
1.4 Product Scope
Urban local bodies and municipal corporations in India routinely receive complaints about civic amenities -- potholes, garbage collection, streetlight outages, water supply, drainage, and instances of corruption or administrative inaction -- yet most corporations still rely on manual registers, phone calls, or in-person visits to log these issues. This results in complaints going unrecorded, no accountability for resolution timelines, difficulty proving that an issue was ever reported, and citizen frustration that discourages future reporting.
The Citizen Grievance Redressal System for Municipal Corporation is a portal that digitizes the entire grievance lifecycle, from citizen reporting (with geo-tagged photographic evidence) through department assignment, officer resolution, and citizen feedback. Its goals are to reduce the friction between citizens and the government when voicing civic problems, create a transparent and time-stamped public record of every complaint, route issues automatically to the correct department, and give citizens visibility into the status of their complaint at every stage. The product is intended to be deployable by a municipal corporation at low operating cost, using cloud-hosted infrastructure and pay-as-you-go third-party services.
1.5 References

The Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, relevant to complaints alleging administrative corruption.

2. Overall Description
2.1 Product Perspective
The Citizen Grievance Redressal System is a new, self-contained, cloud-hosted portal; it is not a follow-on to an existing internal system. Each municipal corporation is provisioned as an isolated tenant, with data further scoped by ward and department within that tenant. The system interacts with three categories of actors: citizens (grievance filers), municipal officers (department-level resolvers), and administrators (corporation-level supervisors), along with supporting third-party services such as the image/media hosting provider, SMS/email notification gateways, and map/geolocation services. At a high level, the platform consists of a citizen-facing web/mobile application, an officer workflow console, an administrator management panel, a department-assignment/routing engine, and a live dashboard and analytics module. These components communicate over encrypted channels and are described further in Section 3.
2.2 Product Functions
At a high level, the platform must allow the system to:
Onboard a municipal corporation, its wards, its departments, and its officer roster.
Let citizens register/log in and file a grievance with a description, category, and one or more geo-tagged photographs of the issue.
Automatically capture the GPS location and timestamp of each submitted photo and pin it on a map.
Route each grievance to the correct department, either automatically (based on category and ward) or via manual reassignment by an administrator.
Allow officers to update grievance status, add resolution remarks and proof-of-resolution photos, and close a grievance.
Let citizens track the real-time status of their grievance from submission through resolution.
Provide a live public/administrative dashboard showing open, in-progress, and resolved grievances by ward, department, and category.
Collect citizen feedback and a satisfaction rating once a grievance is marked resolved, and allow reopening if unresolved.
Send automated status-change notifications to citizens over email/SMS/push notification.
2.3 User Classes and Characteristics
User Class
Description
Key Characteristics

Municipal department staff responsible for investigating and resolving grievances assigned to their department.
Moderate technical expertise; accountable for resolution turnaround time; uses the console regularly.

2.4 Operating Environment
Client: Modern evergreen web browsers (Chrome, Safari, Edge, Firefox -- last two major versions) on desktop and mobile; a responsive React web app usable as the primary mobile experience, with native app packaging (Android 9+ / iOS 14+) considered for a later version.
Server: Cloud-hosted Node.js/Express backend on containerized infrastructure with autoscaling (e.g., AWS/Azure/GCP), deployed within an Indian data-residency-compliant region.
Database: MongoDB (Atlas or self-hosted) for all transactional and geospatial data, using a 2dsphere index for location-based queries.
Media storage: ImageKit for geo-tagged photo upload, storage, transformation, and delivery.
Third-party services: SMS gateway, transactional email service, and a map/geocoding provider, each accessed over secure APIs.
2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints
Budget Constraints:
The system must be operable using free-tier or low-cost cloud infrastructure (MongoDB Atlas, ImageKit, Node hosting) suitable for a municipal corporation pilot deployment.
Third-party SMS/email usage must be minimized or batched to control per-notification cost.
Legal / Statutory Standards:
Must comply with the applicable State Municipal Corporation Act and Right to Public Services Act provisions on time-bound grievance redressal.
Must comply with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and IT Act, 2000 data-security rules for storage and processing of citizen PII and location data.
End-to-end transport encryption is mandatory: TLS 1.3 for data in transit; AES-256 for sensitive data at rest.
Accessibility:
Must support a multilingual UI (English, Hindi, and regional languages relevant to the deploying state) and be usable by citizens with limited digital literacy.
2.6 User Documentation
An online help center / FAQ covering how to register, file a grievance, and track its status.
An Officer & Admin handbook covering department setup, grievance triage, and SLA/escalation rules.
Short in-app walkthroughs (multilingual) for first-time citizens on how to capture and submit a geo-tagged photo.
An API reference document for future integrations with the corporation's existing e-governance systems.
2.7 Assumptions and Dependencies
Assumes the municipal corporation provides an accurate list of wards, departments, and officer assignments prior to go-live.
Assumes citizens' mobile numbers/email addresses are valid and reachable for OTP login and status notifications.
Depends on ImageKit, the SMS/email gateway, and the map/geocoding provider maintaining their published SLAs; an outage in any of these could delay grievance submission or notifications.
Assumes the citizen's device has camera and location-services permissions enabled at the time of filing a grievance; grievances cannot be geo-tagged without this.
Assumes internet connectivity is available to the citizen at the time of filing; an assisted-filing kiosk at ward offices is out of scope for this version unless otherwise noted as a future requirement.
3. External Interface Requirements
3.1 User Interfaces
The platform provides three primary user interfaces, all following a consistent design system, multilingual labeling, and a high-contrast accessibility mode:
Citizen App (web + mobile-responsive): login/registration screen (OTP), grievance-filing form with camera/geo-tag capture, "My Grievances" status-tracking screen, and a feedback/rating screen.
Officer Console (web): assigned-grievance queue, grievance detail view with map location and photos, status-update form with resolution remarks/proof photo, and a personal SLA tracker.
Admin Dashboard (web): live map/heatmap of all grievances, department and officer management, manual reassignment tool, and analytics/export screen.
Every screen must expose a persistent help/support action, display the current language selector, and follow WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility guidelines given the varied digital literacy of the citizen user base.
3.2 Hardware Interfaces
The system has no dedicated hardware of its own. It must interoperate with the standard input hardware of citizens' and officers' devices (camera and GPS/location sensor for geo-tagged photo capture, touchscreen/keyboard for form entry). No specialized municipal hardware is required for this version.

3.4 Communications Interfaces
All client-server communication must occur over HTTPS using TLS 1.3.
REST/HTTPS APIs (JSON payloads) are used for all internal service-to-service and third-party integrations described in Section 3.3.
Push notifications, SMS, and email are used for asynchronous, out-of-band citizen communications regarding grievance status changes.
The system must synchronize server time via NTP to ensure timestamps on grievance submission, status changes, and resolution are accurate and consistent.
4. System Features
4.1 Feature 1: Grievance Filing & Geo-Tagged Reporting
Description & Priority: High Priority. Allows a citizen to report a civic issue with a description, category, and photographic evidence that is automatically geo-tagged.
Stimulus/Response Sequences: Citizen opens app → Logs in via OTP → Fills grievance form (category, description) → Captures photo(s) with location auto-attached → Submits → Receives a tracking ID and confirmation.
Requirement ID
Requirement Title
Description
REQ-GRV-1
Grievance Form Submission
The system must allow a logged-in citizen to submit a grievance with a category, free-text description, ward, and at least one photo.
REQ-GRV-2
Geo-Tagging
The system must automatically capture and attach the device's GPS coordinates and a timestamp to every photo submitted with a grievance.
REQ-GRV-3
Unique Tracking ID
The system shall generate a unique tracking ID for every submitted grievance and display it to the citizen upon submission.
REQ-GRV-4
Duplicate Detection
The system should flag grievances submitted within a configurable radius and time window of an existing open grievance in the same category as a possible duplicate for officer review.
4.2 Feature 2: Live Dashboard & Status Tracking
Description & Priority: High Priority. Gives citizens visibility into their own grievance status and gives officers/admins a real-time operational view of all grievances.

5. Other Nonfunctional Requirements
5.1 Performance Requirements
System Availability: 99.5% uptime, given the platform is used continuously (not restricted to a fixed voting-style window).
Scalability: Capacity to handle at least 5,000 concurrently active users per municipal corporation tenant without noticeable latency.
Response Time: Grievance submission (including photo upload) must complete within 10 seconds under normal network conditions.
5.2 Safety Requirements
Automated backup and disaster recovery protocols to prevent loss of grievance records or associated evidence photos.
5.3 Security Requirements
Data Encryption: TLS 1.3 for data in transit and AES-256 for sensitive data (citizen PII, contact details) at rest.
Authentication: OTP-based login for citizens; role-based access control distinguishing Citizen, Officer, and Admin permissions.
Audit Logging: Every status change and reassignment action must be attributable to an authenticated user and permanently recorded.
5.4 Software Quality Attributes
Accessibility: UI/UX optimized for varying digital literacy, with multilingual support (English, Hindi, and applicable regional languages).
Usability: A citizen must be able to file a grievance, from opening the app to submission, in under 3 minutes.
Reliability: No single point of failure should prevent grievance submission or status viewing during normal operation.
5.5 Business Rules
Transparency: Every grievance and its status history must be viewable by the filing citizen and by administrators for audit purposes.
SLA Compliance: Each department must have a configurable maximum resolution time, with automatic escalation on breach.
Accountability: A grievance cannot be marked resolved without an assigned officer's remark and, where applicable, a proof-of-resolution photo.
6. Other Requirements
Database design must isolate data per municipal-corporation tenant to strictly prevent cross-corporation data leakage, while preserving corporation-wide administrative reporting capabilities.
Appendix A: Glossary
SLA: Service Level Agreement -- the maximum time allowed for a department to resolve a grievance.
OTP: One-Time Password.
Geo-tagging: Attaching geographic location data (latitude/longitude) to a piece of media, such as a photo.
Ward: An administrative sub-division of a municipal corporation used for geographic routing of grievances.
Tenant: An isolated instance of the platform's data belonging to a single municipal corporation.
